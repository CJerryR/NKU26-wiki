03 / Below the surface

The hidden threat.

Above ground, a plant may still look healthy. Below it, an encounter is already unfolding.

  1. Finding a host

    Infective juveniles move through the soil in search of host roots.

  2. Leaving chemical clues

    Nematode activity releases ascarosides. Our project follows two of these signals: ascr#3 and ascr#18.

  3. Entering the roots

    After reaching a host, nematodes invade root tissue and establish a feeding relationship.

  4. Symptoms reach the surface

    Impaired roots can limit water and nutrient uptake. Visible symptoms may follow damage below ground.

Stage 1 of 4 · Finding a host. Select another scene to follow the sequence.

05 / A question to test

Project hypothesis

Could a combination
tell us more?

We focus on ascr#3, ascr#18 and their relationship. Could multiple signals help distinguish background nematode activity from plant-parasitic risk?

That possibility remains under investigation. No signal ratio, decision threshold or discrimination capability is established here.

06 / Our intended design

Turning signals
into colour.

A receptor to recognise. Yeast to relay and amplify. Betaxanthin to make the output visible.

Proposed pathway · under investigation
  1. 01 / Input

    Ascaroside clues

    The chemical signals our project aims to sense.

  2. 02 / Recognition

    A fungal receptor

    A fungus-derived GPCR positioned on the engineered yeast membrane.

  3. 03 / Relay & amplification

    Engineered yeast

    An internal signalling pathway designed to carry and amplify the input.

  4. 04 / Visible output

    Betaxanthin colour

    The intended pigment output, to be tested against appropriate controls.

Explore the engineering ↗

The illustration explains the design goal. A colour response would need validation before it could inform risk pre-screening; it would not be a diagnosis.
