01 - Biology

Life cycle of a hidden parasite

Both target nematodes are sedentary endoparasites: an infective juvenile finds a root, invades it, establishes a feeding site, and matures in place. They represent two forms of this strategy. Heterodera glycines females swell into egg-filled cysts that persist in soil between seasons; Meloidogyne incognita induces galls and giant cells that divert the plant's resources to the parasite (Jones et al., 2013 [1]).

Understanding the cycle helps frame when and where a future sampling workflow might look for a chemical signal. An internal research table identifies the infective second-stage juvenile (J2) as a candidate stage, but the relevant secretion timing still requires primary-source tracing and experimental validation.

Regional profiles differ. Studies listed by the team discuss root-knot nematodes on peppers in Hainan, including M. enterolobii, M. incognita, and M. javanica [2,3], while a separate review examines plant-parasitic nematodes as a food-supply biosecurity risk [4]. These sources motivate regional surveillance, but they do not establish a national economic ranking for M. incognita.

Evidence boundary

Region-specific incidence for the fields relevant to this project has not been supplied and is deliberately not estimated here. Any future regional claim will require a defined geography, sampling basis, and traceable source.

03 - Status quo

Why the problem is not solved

Two things keep this problem open: the pest is hard to remove, and it is hard to catch early. The first is a matter of biology and control; the second is the diagnostic gap our project targets (covered in Description).

Persists in soil

Persistent eggs and cysts complicate management between growing seasons.

Patchy distribution

Nematode aggregation in soil can make a small sample unrepresentative of the wider field (Jiang et al., 2025) [6].

Detection trade-offs

Existing diagnostic methods trade off speed, equipment, expertise, specificity, and sampling reliability.

Where this leads

The hidden life cycle and sampling uncertainty motivate investigation of an earlier chemical-sensing concept. Before such evidence could guide a grower decision, representative sampling, candidate-biomarker persistence, receptor response, decision thresholds, agronomic interpretation, and a responsible deployment workflow would all need validation.
