# Sustainability

01 - Why

## Detection is not automatically sustainable

Earlier information could, in principle, support more timely and targeted crop-protection decisions. That pathway is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated outcome of this project. No reviewed evidence shows that the proposed sensor reduces crop loss, avoids chemical use, prevents waste, changes management, or performs in soil.

A sustainability claim must connect a reliable measurement to an available action and then compare the full consequences with current practice. Sampling effort, false results, repeat testing, equipment, energy, biological containment, consumables, transport, data interpretation, and disposal can all move burdens rather than remove them. The project has not yet completed that comparison.

02 - SDGs

## Potential pathways, not achieved outcomes

### Food-system relevance

Plant-parasitic nematodes are a documented agricultural burden, so better diagnostic evidence could be relevant to crop resilience. The team has not measured yield protection or food-security impact, and a broad problem statement does not establish the benefit of this detector.

### Responsible inputs

A test could support more selective action only if its target, sampling, specificity, decision threshold, and follow-up response are valid. No evidence currently shows reduced nematicide use or another input benefit. False reassurance and unnecessary treatment must be included in future evaluation.

### Equitable access

Access is not established by calling a tool portable. A future assessment must examine sample collection, equipment, training, language, result interpretation, cost, maintenance, and who can act on the answer. No smallholder suitability or distribution advantage has been validated.

03 - Footprint

## Measure the project as well as the promise

The wiki is a static site with locally hosted assets and no runtime third-party dependency, which limits one category of operational complexity. We have not quantified its energy or carbon footprint, so the implementation should not be presented as a measured environmental benefit.

The larger footprint lies in laboratory and future assay choices. A useful inventory would record material and energy inputs, cold or ambient storage, failed batches, hazardous and biological waste, packaging, transport, equipment lifetime, repeat-test rates, and end-of-life handling. Alternative designs should be compared on the same functional unit and include uncertainty.

**Current documented status**

No life-cycle assessment, field outcome, avoided-impact calculation, or verified sustainability benefit is documented. The responsible next step is to define the comparison, gather operational data, publish trade-offs, and revise the design when evidence shows that a claimed benefit shifts harm elsewhere.
