Teaching records, with honest limits

The available records establish two documented activities and the teaching materials prepared for them. They support an account of what the courses were designed to explain, but they contain no verified attendance or reach count and no measured learning impact. We therefore describe the teaching structure without treating delivery records as evidence that understanding changed.

Across both activities, the instructional goal was to make an invisible biological problem discussable. The materials move from familiar observations and comparisons toward a simplified detection chain, while keeping the team's unvalidated detector concept separate from established science and from classroom scenarios.

Two documented teaching designs

Nematode Chase

On 14 May 2026, the activity used a 57-slide deck built as a farm mystery. Learners were cast as investigators who followed clues, used tool cards, made choices, encountered alternate outcomes, and reflected on their route. The deck organized the journey around trail capture, signal relay, signal display, testing and reflection, and review, then introduced DBTL as a way to revisit a design.

Tracing Worm Signs

On 26 May 2026, a 30-slide deck introduced nematode diversity through comparison and observation. Its notes distinguish harmful and beneficial roles, compare nematodes with more familiar animals, introduce iGEM and synthetic biology, and connect signals to an observable change. Detection examples appear as teaching material; image-led slides and optional demonstrations are not described beyond what the reviewed notes establish.

Instructional choices

Narrative framing gave the first deck a question to pursue rather than a list to memorize. Comparison and observation connected unfamiliar organisms with visible examples. Discussion prompts made uncertainty explicit. The second recap separated detection into a signal source, a recognition method, and an observable readout, allowing each step to be questioned without implying that the complete system already works.

What the records do not measure

No verified attendance or reach count, participant age breakdown, or exact session duration is present in the reviewed records. No questionnaire or pre/post test is documented. No participant feedback summary, learning-outcome analysis, behaviour change, or follow-up engagement is documented either.

No participant photographs are published on this page. Photograph publication authorization remains unverified, so activity images are retained only as evidence-review material and are not used here.

Evidence ceiling

The decks and their scenarios are teaching material, not proof of a working detector, species discrimination, sensor performance, or end-to-end validation. Classroom success endings explain a design story; they do not establish an experimental result for the team.
