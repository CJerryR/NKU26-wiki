Our work: dry lab and wet lab

The wet lab builds a yeast that turns an ascaroside signal into color. The dry lab screens its receptor and turns that color into nematode numbers and advice.

Point at any step to read about it.

  1. Wet lab: Arthrobotrys oligospora, a nematode-trapping fungus, captures the nematodes around it.
  2. Wet lab: in it we found a receptor that recognizes ascarosides, the nematodes' chemical signals.
  3. Dry lab: we took this receptor and designed variants of it.
  4. Dry lab: molecular docking and molecular dynamics screened the variants that recognize ascr#3 and ascr#18.
  5. Wet lab: the modified gene goes into yeast, which then carries the receptor and recognizes ascarosides.
  6. Wet lab: the receptor signal is coupled to the yeast's own pathway and switches on the betaxanthin synthesis genes.
  7. Wet lab: betaxanthin builds up inside and outside the cells and the culture medium changes color.
  8. Wet lab: OD and RGB measurements of the medium give the pigment concentration.
  9. Dry lab: a lumped model links pigment concentration to the ascaroside concentration, and so to nematode activity: N nematode = f(OD, t, …) = f′(color).
  10. Dry lab: the eco model takes the abundance of harmful nematodes, estimates the economic impact and suggests what to do.
