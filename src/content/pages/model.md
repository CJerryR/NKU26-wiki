## Model {#model-overview toc="Model"}

<p class="author-source-link"><a href="/source-archive/#meeting-20261019" data-source-document="meeting-20261019-7eafd8795b38">Original submission (.docx)</a><span role="status" aria-live="polite"></span></p>

<section class="author-manuscript" lang="en">

:::lead

<p style="text-align:left;"> How can nematode chemical signals in soil be converted into useful detection information? Our dry-lab work addresses this question by connecting receptor engineering, dynamic structural analysis, signal and color-development simulations, and economic evaluation, progressively exploring a path from ascaroside recognition to agricultural management. </p>

:::

:::cards{cols="2"}

### Docking | Identifying engineering directions for differential responses to two ligands {href="/model-docking/"}

<p>We compared receptor docking scores and contact distributions for ascr#18 and ascr#3, screening candidates through alanine substitutions, saturation scanning, and combination tests. We selected Gpr2 N84V and V368R, which showed shifts toward different ligands, for further study. Cross-receptor sequence and structural comparisons provided additional residue positions for parallel testing.</p>

### Molecular Dynamics | Observing candidate poses in a membrane environment {href="/model-md/"}

<p>Starting from docked conformations, we simulated receptor–ligand complexes in a membrane environment and jointly analyzed receptor motion, ligand positioning, and contact rearrangements. These dynamic observations help interpret candidate structural behavior and turn static docking clues into more specific experimental questions.</p>

### ODE and Dual-Channel Models | From signal input to target density estimation {href="/model-ode/"}

<p>We used ODEs to connect effective receptor input, yeast signaling, and color development, then converted simulated pigment outputs into smartphone color features. Using two complementary responses, the dual-channel model explores how to estimate southern root-knot nematode density when background nematode abundance is unknown, providing a framework for experimental calibration.</p>

### Eco | Evaluating the practical value of density information {href="/model-eco/"}

<p>Using target density as the interface, the economic model connects potential yield loss, testing costs, and treatment costs. It compares net returns from no treatment, uniform treatment, and testing followed by tiered treatment to examine when detection information can change management choices and generate benefits.</p>

:::

<p style="text-align:left;"> Together, the four components form a research pathway: <strong> Receptor engineering proposes recognition hypotheses; dynamic simulations add structural interpretations; signaling and dual-channel models explore density inference; and the economic model evaluates the value of information. </strong> Docking and MD inform sensor design. ODEs, the dual-channel model, and Eco connect through color-development and density interfaces, while the precision needed for management tiers feeds back into subsequent detection calibration. </p>
