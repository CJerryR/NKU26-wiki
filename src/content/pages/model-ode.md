## From signal response to target density {#ode-introduction toc="Two connected models"}

After receptors recognize ascarosides, two further transformations are needed: intracellular signaling must produce readable color, and that color must inform an estimate of target-nematode density. In a mixture of root-knot and free-living nematodes, stronger color could reflect more target organisms or a changing background.

We connect an ODE model of effective receptor input, the shared yeast pathway and pigment formation with a **two-channel density model**. ODE simulations generate responses to mixtures; paired colors then estimate target density without requiring the background count. We first ask whether the pathway preserves input differences, then whether those differences distinguish target from background.

## Choosing which dynamics to retain {#ode-minimal toc="Minimal model"}

Our initial chain was ligand → receptor → G protein → MAPK → synthetic transcription factor (sTF) → GAL → color. To retain the chain while limiting parameter count, we used **three dynamic equations** for G protein, MAPK and color, with receptor response, sTF activity and GAL output represented algebraically.

$b_R$ and $b_T$ describe basal activity; $E_R$ and $E_T$ describe response amplitudes. The $K$ values and Hill exponents shape the responses. $N$ is the copy number in a fixed construct; $\ell$, $g$ and $D_T$ describe GAL output; $p$ and $\delta$ control color production and loss. In this first model, $C$ was relative color and time was in hours.

The model located every functional stage but exposed a parameter-identification problem. Input sensitivity, signaling amplification and pigment efficiency all affect final color, and a single endpoint cannot easily identify their separate effects. We therefore reused published quantitative reactions and parameters for the shared yeast pathway, retaining an effective description of the project-specific pigment stage. Calibration could then focus on input and output interfaces.

## Connecting the published core to pigment production {#ode-core toc="Six-state model"}

Using Supady Model B and public TU Delft 2012 iGEM materials [1,2], we reconstructed the G-protein cycle, scaffold module, Fus3 activation and feedback. Unlike the initial two-variable summary, this core retains source-model reactions and records internal rates separately from project-specific parameters.

Conservation of molecular pools allows some states to be reconstructed. We retain five independent variables: G-protein heterotrimer $H$, activated Gα $A$, effective scaffold complex $Q$, activated Fus3 $F^*$ and activated feedback state $Z^*$:

Conservation removes redundant states; merging pigment output focuses endpoint constraints on a small number of effective parameters. The Fus3-to-pigment interface still requires **experimental calibration**.

## Constructing two inputs from independent populations {#ode-mixtures toc="Target and background"}

The target is *Meloidogyne incognita* (Mi), and *Caenorhabditis elegans* (Ce) provides the background. Both can contribute ascr#3 and ascr#18, so both ligands are retained while the two organism densities vary independently.

| Simulation setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Combined release $q_3+q_{18}$ | 1 fmol per nematode per hour for both Mi and Ce |
| Release ratio $q_3:q_{18}$ | Mi 1:10; Ce 10:1 |
| Input weights $(w_3,w_{18})$ | P3: (2/3, 1/3); P18: (1/3, 2/3) |

Published secretion profiles motivate compositional differences [3,4]. The exact ratios, equal combined release and response weights in this table construct an **idealized simulation scenario**; they are **not measured secretion rates** from our samples. Mi is represented by J2 and Ce by L4, with density per 100 cm³ soil. Collection time and effective volume are fixed in the parameter table.

Each ligand concentration combines the two populations' contributions, collection time and effective volume. Holding target density fixed while adding background examines interference; holding background fixed while adding target examines target response.

The complementary P3- and P18-biased designs correspond to the candidate directions of V368R and N84V. Docking motivates this design, but pure-ligand gradient experiments must establish actual weights. Cross-response is retained, so both colors reflect the mixture. This also defines a measured calibration design: independently vary target and background, record paired colors from each sample, and include blanks and different background levels.

## Calibrating input and output interfaces {#ode-calibration toc="Endpoint calibration"}

In early simulations, different stimulus strengths produced similar final colors. We therefore held the shared pathway fixed and adjusted effective input scale $K_L$ and pigment gain $a$. A larger $K_L$ weakens effective drive at the same stimulus; $a$ controls pigment amplitude. Each sensor fitted these two parameters while keeping $K$, $n$ and $\delta$ fixed.

Six Mi densities crossed with three Ce backgrounds gave **18 simulated endpoint targets**, covering low density, grading boundaries and higher density. Targets combined prespecified ligand contributions with a shared color scale. We then sought interface parameters that approached them.

The calibrated ODE broadly followed the targets but retained a sublinear response at weak stimulation. The next question was whether this low-end bias prevents useful density estimation. We therefore used the **actual ODE output**, including its deviations, for the two-channel evaluation.

:::note[Two distinct calibration steps]
Prespecified color endpoints calibrate the ODE. Colors calculated by the ODE train the two-channel density model. Prespecified linear targets do not replace the ODE's computed results.
:::

To connect to the phone-reading example, we subsequently set 8 h total-pigment targets to 0.07–0.56 mg/mL indicaxanthin equivalent, adjusted basal production and uniformly scaled net pigment amplitude. Production coefficients were adjusted analytically while retaining $K_L$, then checked by full ODE integration.

The two channels' actual endpoints cover approximately **0.070–0.543** and **0.070–0.480 mg/mL**. The endpoint-fit RMSE across 18 points is about **0.0097 mg/mL** for both. This changes output scale while preserving response shape and relative deviations. The 0.07 mg/mL blank is a chosen scenario value; real yeast background and induced yields await measurement.

$j$ is P3 or P18; $B$ is the image blue-channel value; $c$ is in mg/mL. This simulation uses $k_B=0.36$ mL/mg. Taking the ratio to each channel's own blank describes its color change; the shared calibrated intercept cancels after subtraction.

We calculate $r_j$ from the ODE pigment output and then obtain $x_j$ for the density model. These are **simulated blue-channel ratios**. Exposure, scattering and device differences require calibration using standards and paired sample measurements.

The ODE calculates pigment formation; the phone relationship describes its observation. Separating them allows a measured phone-calibration curve to replace the observation interface without redefining the signaling model.

Under these simulation conditions, **the second channel reduces density error**. A single color responds to target and background, so similar colors can represent different mixtures. Different response weights let the combined model distinguish more of that variation.

For Mi=40 and Ce=80, simulated sample-to-blank blue ratios are approximately 0.854 and 0.879. The model estimates Mi at approximately **39.72 J2/100 cm³**, which enters the middle management band of the Eco example without requiring Ce density at prediction time.

Near grading thresholds, small density deviations can change the selected band. The interface therefore checks calibration range and indicates neighboring scenarios when an estimate interval crosses a threshold.

These results come from noiseless simulations under the same ODE generator. They describe internal estimation under assumed secretion, sensing and observation relationships, rather than field validation. Density predictions remain essentially unchanged after adding the phone interface, showing that the new observation representation retains the earlier information.

Code, parameter sources, frozen configurations and sample data support reproduction and later incorporation of measured paired data. [Eco](/model-eco/) examines how density information changes management choices and conditional returns.
