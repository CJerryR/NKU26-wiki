## From Hidden Soil Risks to Shared Scientific Learning {#from-hidden-soil-risks-to-shared-scientific-learning toc="From Hidden Soil Risks to Shared Scientific Learning"}

Plant-parasitic nematodes live below the soil surface. By the time damage becomes visible above ground, the roots may already be seriously affected. Our iGEM project explores early warning for agricultural nematode risks, but developing a detection idea is only one part of addressing this challenge. People also need accessible ways to understand the problem, the science behind possible solutions, and the questions that remain open.

We therefore connected our project with science education for young learners. Our starting point was not simply to introduce a technology, but to make an easily overlooked agricultural issue understandable and to invite young people to explore how scientific evidence can help reveal what is happening beneath the soil.

## Listening to the Context {#listening-to-the-context toc="Listening to the Context"}

Before and alongside our outreach, our team investigated public awareness and practical needs related to plant-parasitic nematodes. The fieldwork report records more than 370 valid questionnaire responses collected across 17 provincial-level administrative regions, together with expert consultation and visits to agricultural settings. In the survey, 94.58% of respondents considered nematode damage to have a substantial impact on agricultural production. The report also describes a gap between the hidden nature of the disease and people’s ability to recognize it early.

These findings helped us explain why the topic matters beyond the laboratory. They are context for our educational work, rather than a measurement of what students learned in our classes. We brought the project’s agricultural context into outreach and developed three complementary course series for different age groups.

## Three Course Series {#three-course-series toc="Three Course Series"}

Our educational program consists of three course series: a theoretical science course, an experimental science course, and a values-oriented course. The theoretical and experimental courses each have primary- and middle-school versions. The values-oriented course currently has a primary-school version.

### Theoretical science course: The Nematode Chase {#theoretical-science-course-the-nematode-chase toc="Theoretical science course: The Nematode Chase"}

**Audience:** Primary- and middle-school students

**Duration:** Two class periods

This course introduces plant-parasitic nematodes and the agricultural problem at the center of our project. Its title frames the topic as an investigation: learners are invited to think about an organism that is difficult to notice and why early detection matters. The two age-specific versions allow the subject to be introduced at different school levels.

The course is supported by a leaflet, postcards, a picture book, and posters. These materials provide several formats for introducing the project and its scientific context.

### Experimental science course: The Signal Exploration Challenge {#experimental-science-course-the-signal-exploration-challenge toc="Experimental science course: The Signal Exploration Challenge"}

**Audience:** Primary- and middle-school students

**Duration:** One class period

This course introduces students to the idea of detecting and interpreting a signal. It connects with the project’s early-warning theme and offers an entry point to experimental science and biosensing. The course has separate primary- and middle-school versions.

### Values-oriented course: Small Lives, Bright Light: Growing Through Ordinary Perseverance {#values-oriented-course-small-lives-bright-light-growing-through-ordinary-perseverance toc="Values-oriented course: Small Lives, Bright Light: Growing Through Ordinary Perseverance"}

**Audience:** Primary-school students

**Duration:** One class period

This course extends the program beyond scientific concepts. Its title connects attention to small or easily overlooked forms of life with reflection on perseverance and growth. It complements the two science courses with a values-oriented perspective.

## Bringing the Courses to Learners {#bringing-the-courses-to-learners toc="Bringing the Courses to Learners"}

The collected outreach materials document course activities in Gansu, Hebei, Jiangxi, and Tianjin, including school-based teaching and community activities. The fieldwork report also records outreach across Beijing, Tianjin, Jiangxi, Hebei, and Gansu through the team’s collaboration with the “向阳计划” program. Together, the course versions and supporting materials were developed for continued use in science outreach and educational activities.

Our practice report describes the courses as resources produced through a wider project journey involving surveys, expert conversations, and visits to agricultural settings. This gave the education program a concrete agricultural context: early warning matters because nematode damage can remain hidden, while possible solutions must ultimately respond to real production needs.
