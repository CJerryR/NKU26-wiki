<p>From contacting alumni enterprises and preparing partnership materials to meeting companies and seeking commercialization advice, we made industry engagement part of our exploration of the project’s potential applications. Confirmed sponsorship supported our work, while the materials and outreach process we developed became <strong>valuable outcomes</strong> in their own right.</p>

:::

## Starting with email outreach {#hp-source-229}

<p>We contacted multiple companies by email and reached out widely to alumni enterprises for their interest and advice. Our initial messages introduced the team, our agricultural nematode early-warning project, and the purpose of the proposed exchange. Project materials helped explain the problem we hoped to address. As communication progressed, we coordinated with company representatives to arrange presentations and meetings.</p>

<p>Our outreach covered sponsorship opportunities as well as advice on applications, industry needs, and possible commercialization pathways. Where a sponsorship did not follow, the exchange could still provide useful professional perspectives.</p>

## Preparing materials for industry discussions {#hp-source-233}

<p>We prepared a corporate partnership invitation and a project presentation to communicate our work clearly. The invitation brought together our team background, project rationale, application needs, possible forms of collaboration, and follow-up arrangements. The presentation provided a visual introduction to the sensor concept, our Human Practices activities, and potential applications. Together, they supported both detailed review and meeting presentations.</p>

<p>The invitation outlined possible financial sponsorship, in-kind or service support, and industry engagement, alongside a proposed cooperation process and partner recognition. The presentation explored a field-testing concept for potential users such as agricultural input companies, large farms, and crop protection service providers, as well as longer-term possibilities for a broader sensing platform. These were directions for discussion and exploration; product development requires further research and validation.</p>

## Moving from presentations to dialogue {#hp-source-239}

<p>Following initial outreach and preparation, we held meetings with several companies to present our progress and seek advice on applications and commercialization. These exchanges offered perspectives beyond the laboratory and an opportunity to discuss potential users, how to communicate the value of the proposed test, and the conditions that would need consideration before moving toward practical use.</p>

<p>We approached these discussions as an exploration of possible application pathways. Companies could contribute through experience sharing, discussion of needs, resource connections, or sponsorship. We record and acknowledge these different contributions separately.</p>

## Developing a reusable outreach process {#hp-source-247}

:::lead

<p>Through this work, we developed <strong>a process that can inform future outreach</strong>: define the purpose of engagement, establish contact by email, share the invitation and project materials, hold discussions around company interests, and follow up on collaboration opportunities and support. The invitation, presentation, and communication records provide a foundation for this process.</p>

<p>For other student teams, industry engagement can begin with a concrete question: what do we hope to learn from a company, and what help are we seeking? Recording consultation, expressions of interest, and confirmed support separately makes progress easier to communicate and helps acknowledge each contribution accurately.</p>

:::
