01 — Documented

What the records actually show

The audited notebook documents a set of standard microbiology and molecular-biology practices. We describe them here as documented practices only — they are what the records show the team doing, not proof of full biosafety compliance, which would require the institutional information listed further down this page.

Chassis handled

Trans5α competent E. coli and S. cerevisiae CEN.PK2-1C, as named in the records.

Bench practices

Autoclaving wrapped, labelled vessels with indicator tape; aseptic hood and clean bench for plating and cell work.

Recorded precautions

Light and UV protection for tryptophan-containing material; minimal (~10 s) UV during gel excision; deliberate marker labelling.

Also documented across the records: plasmid handling and minipreps, PCR and agarose-gel electrophoresis, and yeast transformation with dropout selection. None of these entries, on its own, demonstrates that a formal safety framework was in place.

03 — Antibiotics

An antibiotic value to confirm first

The earliest records (0506 and 0507) state an ampicillin stock and working concentration that are atypical for standard LB–ampicillin selection and may reflect a unit error. The suspect values are not printed here. They must be confirmed with the team before any protocol or risk assessment that depends on antibiotic handling is finalised. The corresponding bench workflow is documented on the Protocols page, also with the numbers withheld.

04 — By design

Containment and non-release as future requirements

The intended direction of the project is a diagnostic that reads a soil-borne nematode signal rather than an organism released into the environment. That is a design intention, not a demonstrated safeguard: the current records show no direct-soil testing, environmental release, field deployment, or finished diagnostic enclosure.

Non-release is a requirement, not a result

Physical and biological containment and strict non-release would be requirements for any future implementation of this system. Nothing in the present records demonstrates them, so we describe them as design obligations to meet, not features already achieved.

05 — Risk

Risks to weigh in future design

Because no device has been built or tested, the following are prospective design risks rather than measured performance. A future assay would need to address the possibility of false positives and false negatives, and the specificity of the chosen biomarker: literature the team reviewed describes the candidate ascaroside as conserved across several related root-knot species, which may prevent species-level discrimination; its full taxonomic range and specificity remain untested. Turning that into a reliable readout — and defining any real-world sensitivity, specificity, or response behaviour — remains future design work.

No measured device performance

This page reports no detection limit, response time, sensitivity, specificity, or species discrimination. Those are not established by the records and are not claimed here.
