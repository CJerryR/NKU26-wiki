01 - Users

Who might use future evidence

The groups below are intended-user hypotheses, not documented adopters. Future human-practices work must test their actual decisions, constraints, and interpretation needs before the project defines a user workflow.

Growers

A possible audience if representative sampling and an interpretable result can be validated for a real crop-management decision.

Agronomists & advisors

A possible interpretation layer between an assay result and any recommendation; no decision threshold or agronomic meaning is established.

Extension & co-ops

Potential coordinators of sampling or communication, subject to evidence about training, quality control, access, and responsibility.

02 - Delivery

A conditional path from sample to decision

No product form, kit, assay time, storage condition, supply chain, or manufacturing route has been validated. Implementation therefore begins with questions, not a deployment promise.

A future pathway would first need a representative sampling method and a defined way to prepare a soil-derived input. It would then need direct evidence that the candidate receptor responds to the intended ligand, evidence that the engineered pathway couples that response, and a selected reporter with bounded background and output behaviour.

Only after those component gates could an end-to-end test ask whether the result is repeatable in controlled samples and then in an appropriate soil matrix. Interpretation would require a justified threshold, controls, uncertainty handling, and evidence connecting the readout to a decision. None of those links currently exists.

Form factor, operator steps, equipment, storage, transport, waste handling, and quality assurance remain open design decisions. They should be specified only after biological feasibility and intended-user needs are supported by evidence.

03 - Regulation

Containment before deployment

The proposed chassis is living engineered yeast. It must not be assumed suitable for release into soil or the wider environment. Any future implementation would require a containment strategy, verified inactivation, defined disposal, exposure assessment, biosafety review, and a jurisdiction-specific regulatory assessment.

These gates must be evaluated alongside biological performance rather than after it. A useful result would also need controls, traceability, operator guidance, and a clear boundary on what the output can and cannot establish.

Engagement is a validation gate

Internal records show exploratory consultation and planning, but not a public stakeholder-to-design change loop. Future engagement should be consented, attributable where appropriate, and documented against specific design decisions.

04 - Roadmap

Evidence before scale

  • Current evidence

    Component-level attempts

    Notebook records support molecular workflows, negative and inconclusive checks, and a preliminary delta-Gal80 PCR interpretation without sequence confirmation. Receptor response, reporter output, and end-to-end operation are not demonstrated.

  • Next gates

    Biological feasibility

    Trace the candidate-analyte evidence; test receptor expression, localisation, and ligand response; establish pathway coupling; select a reporter; and measure controlled end-to-end behaviour with matched controls.

  • Later gates

    Responsible use assessment

    Only after biological feasibility should the team evaluate representative sampling, soil-matrix effects, quantitative performance, intended-user interpretation, containment, waste, biosafety, regulation, manufacturing feasibility, and whether deployment is justified.
