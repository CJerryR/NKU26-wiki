Judges reading this wiki should not have to hunt for the evidence behind a claim. This page
lists each medal criterion and each special prize we are entering, says where the supporting
work lives, and states plainly how far that work has actually reached.

Two of our practices are unusual and worth flagging before the tables. First, we tag claims by
evidence level throughout the wiki, so a reader can always tell literature from assumption
from our own measurement. Second, where a criterion is not yet met, this page says so rather
than linking to a page that implies otherwise.

## How to read the evidence tags {#evidence-tags data-toc="Evidence tags"}

Every substantive claim on this wiki carries one of four tags. They are not decoration: they
are how we keep the distinction that our narrative brief asks for, between what the literature
reports, what we assume, and what our own records show.

<Evidence level="literature">
**Literature.** Reported in a publication we cite at that point in the text. The citation
links to the reference list at the foot of the page.
</Evidence>

<Evidence level="hypothesis">
**Hypothesis.** A project assumption. It shapes our design, but we have not tested it, and no
result on this wiki depends on it being true.
</Evidence>

<Evidence level="result">
**Team result.** Observed in our own records, with the record identified. Negative and
inconclusive results carry this tag too — they are results.
</Evidence>

<Evidence level="open">
**Not yet shown.** Evidence a claim would need before we could make it. We list these
explicitly rather than leaving the gap for a judge to discover.
</Evidence>

## Special prizes {#prizes data-toc="Special prizes"}

We are entering Best Integrated Human Practices, plus two prizes chosen from Model, Part
Collection and Education. Each block below states the claim, then points at the evidence.
Until the underlying work is complete, these entries describe the shape of the argument rather
than asserting the result.

### Best Integrated Human Practices {#ihp}

Our brief for this work is a three-question loop, and the iHP page is organised around it
rather than around a list of meetings: can the signal be seen, can it be explained, and can it
support a decision. The case rests on showing a specific project change for a specific piece
of outside input, so each entry on that page ends with what it changed and a link to the
changed thing.

- Framework and stakeholder map: [Human Practices](page:human-practices)
- Design changes traced to outside input: [Engineering](page:engineering-cycle) and [Description](page:description)
- Where the tool would sit in a real workflow — sampling, pre-screening, professional
  confirmation, management decision: [Human Practices](page:human-practices)

### Model {#model-prize}

The modelling work is being written to state what each model is for, what it assumes, and
what it can and cannot say, with validation data identified as separate from any data used to
fit it. See [Model](page:modeling).

### Part Collection {#parts-prize}

Registry documentation is the evidence for this prize and cannot be replaced by a wiki page;
the wiki side is an index with the current status of each element. See [Parts](page:parts).

### Education {#education-prize}

The case here is evidence that participants' understanding changed, not attendance counts. See
[Education](page:education).
