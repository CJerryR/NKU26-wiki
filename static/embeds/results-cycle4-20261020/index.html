02Quality spot colours, standard shows no colour at all
 not usable
Discard the card.
 +–

The standard is the only spot whose answer is known in advance, so it is what turns a colour into a number under that day's conditions. Losing it is rare — it would mean the reagent was never deposited, or degraded in a unit stored too warm — but it cannot be worked around: with nothing to calibrate against, the card is silent. The quality spot cannot stand in, because it is saturated and only ever says "alive".
03The whole card looks yellow; blank darker than the paper

Leachate and cells add a common background over every spot. Anything common is subtracted once by the software; the result is read as a ratio, so a uniform tint changes nothing. This is the case where the card looks wrong and is not.

The two units carry receptors for two different ascarosides, released in different proportions by free-living and by plant-parasitic nematodes. The difference is the measurement, not a fault — which is why neither spot is judged on its own depth.
05Dust or a fibre on a spot  |  ink or a scratch across it
brush off discard
Brush it off and re-shoot / discard.
 +–

A particle resting on the surface only shades the spot — it can be removed and the spot underneath is untouched. A stain or a cut has entered the paper and changed the cell layer, so the colour no longer reports what the cells did. Same appearance, different verdict: what matters is whether the damage sits on the card or in it.
06Reference strip has faded — the three tones have flattened
 not usable
Discard; store away from light.
 +–

The three patches are the only fixed reference in the photograph, and their values are known from the laboratory. Light does not shift them, it flattens them: the three tones drift toward one another, which is visible without any comparison — unlike a colour cast, which nobody can judge by eye without a reference. Once flat, they can no longer separate illumination from the card's own colour.
